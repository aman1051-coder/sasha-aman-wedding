Our Story

From strangers to forever
The Beginning
July 1, 2019
◆

A boy from the streets of Delhi and a girl shaped by the salt and soul of Goa — two people who should have remained strangers somehow found their way to the same moment. Not with fireworks, but with something quieter and more lasting: the ease of talking to someone who simply gets it. In the beginning, it was long messages, shared playlists, and finding excuses to extend plans by just ten more minutes.

"What stood out wasn't the spark. It was the comfort — the feeling that you could exhale completely, for the first time."

Through overlapping timezones, countless flights, and a world that locked down around us, we chose each other every single day. There were trips and plans, small disagreements about directions, Sunday mornings that turned into lazy afternoons, and the slow steady rhythm of two people building something real. No grand declarations. Just consistency. And eventually — certainty.

The Decision
◆

Somewhere along the way, this stopped being a story about two people who met — and became a story about a life taking shape. A shared language. Inside jokes that would take hours to explain. A future that made more sense together than apart.

This December, the distance closes permanently. We return to Goa — her home, the place that first gave her the sea and the sunsets — to weave our heritages together. Standing by the water, we'll make the same promises we've been quietly keeping for years. Out loud, this time. In front of everyone we love.

You are part of our story too

Every gathering you came to with us, every late night you heard too much about each other, every time you asked "so when's the wedding?" — this is your answer.

We couldn't imagine it without you.

Getting Dressed

Dress the celebration

We want you to feel beautiful, comfortable, and utterly yourself. Here's a guide for each moment — curated inspiration, not a strict dress code.

Day One  ·  4th December 2026
Holy Matrimony
3:00 PM — Church Ceremony
Marriage Nuptials

This one calls for formal and modest: think covered shoulders, thoughtful lengths, and an elegance that photographs beautifully in the pews. A light shawl or stole keeps things demure inside the church.

Formal & Modest — Church Ceremony

Women: Cocktail dress or elegant gown, with a shawl for the church  ·  Men: A tailored suit for the ceremony

6:00 PM Onward — Reception
Reception

No wardrobe change is required — the ceremony look eases naturally into golden hour. That said, an outfit change is more than welcome if you'd like to switch into something more open, as per your liking — just be mindful that time must permit it. Shawl off, touch-up done, and now the fun begins — both women and men are free to dress it up, loosen up, and lean into the evening.

Semi-Formal to Festive — Change Optional

Women: Stay in your look, or elevate into something more evening-ready — entirely your call  ·  Men: Lose the jacket, roll up the sleeves, dress it up or down as you like

Wear whatever you like, just not white. We already have a bride, we don't need an understudy.

Day Two  ·  5th December 2026
Hindu Ceremonies
9:30 AM – 2 PM — Pooja, Mehendi & Marigolds
Morning Rituals

Loose, breathable, cheerful — you'll be sitting for henna and celebrating with turmeric, so comfort comes first. This function's palette is shades of pink and green.

Festive Casual — Pink & Green, Haldi-Safe

Women: Cotton kurtas or lehengas in pink & green  ·  Men: Green or pink kurtas — not your best outfit, turmeric stains!

4:00 PM Onward — Baarat, Pheras & Gala
Baarat to Gala

One vibrant evening from start to finish — the Baarat procession, the sacred Pheras, and the Gala that follows all flow together, so dress in your finest festive Indian wear from the very start. Rich fabrics, embroidery, the heirloom sari you've been saving — maximum colour, maximum joy, and you're set for the whole night.

Festive to Traditional Indian Wear

Women: Anarkali, lehenga, or sari in rich festive colours  ·  Men: Sherwani or bandhgala, colourful and celebratory  ·  Avoid white, black & red (reserved for the bride)
